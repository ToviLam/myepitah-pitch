The Digital Divide of Grief

In a hyper-connected world, a person leaves behind two trails. Families are forced to mourn while simultaneously deciphering passwords, scattered final wishes, and hidden accounts — often at the hardest possible time.

The Emotional Legacy

Sentiments, guidance, memories, and farewell messages meant for loved ones — too often lost, or left unspoken.

The Practical Reality

Locked accounts, forgotten subscriptions, scattered documents, and funeral wishes no one had written down.

Privacy by Design

Yours, and only yours

Your most sensitive words are encrypted before they ever leave your device, and your data is stored in Hong Kong in line with local privacy law. Even we cannot read what you write. That isn't a feature — it's the foundation.

Wabi Tov Studio

There is a Japanese word — 侘 — that finds beauty in the imperfect. The tea bowl worn smooth by ten thousand mornings. The branch that bent under snow and grew more interesting for it. Wabi does not ask things to be unmarked. It asks them to be looked at honestly — and in that honest looking, something shifts.

There is a Hebrew word — טוֹב — that is the other half of this. It means good. Wholly, completely, declaratively good. Not despite what a thing is, but because of what it is. It does not find goodness by lowering expectations. It declares the goodness that was already there.

This is how we build. Carefully, honestly, and with full attention — shipping nothing we would be ashamed to leave behind ourselves. MyEpitaph is the first thing we're making.
